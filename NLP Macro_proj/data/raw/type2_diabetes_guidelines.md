# Clinical Standards of Care in Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

**Source:** American Diabetes Association & European Association for the Study of Diabetes Standards  
**Document Code:** ADA-EASD-T2D-2024  
**Scope:** Adult Endocrinology and Primary Care  

---

## 1. Diagnostic Criteria for Diabetes and Prediabetes

Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2D) is characterized by progressive loss of adequate beta-cell insulin secretion frequently on the background of insulin resistance.

Diagnostic confirmation requires meeting at least one of the following criteria (confirmed by repeat testing in the absence of unequivocal hyperglycemia):
1. **Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG):** ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) after an overnight fast of at least 8 hours.
2. **2-Hour Post-Load Plasma Glucose (2-h PG):** ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) during a standardized 75g Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT).
3. **Glycated Hemoglobin (HbA1c):** ≥ 6.5% (48 mmol/mol) using a certified NGSP-standardized laboratory assay.
4. **Random Plasma Glucose:** ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) in a patient with classic symptoms of hyperglycemia (polyuria, polydipsia, unexplained weight loss, fatigue) or hyperglycemic crisis.

*Prediabetes Thresholds:* FPG 100–125 mg/dL (impaired fasting glucose), 2-h PG 140–199 mg/dL (impaired glucose tolerance), or HbA1c 5.7–6.4%.

## 2. Risk Factors and Pathophysiology

Major predisposing factors include:
* **Overweight and Central Adiposity:** BMI ≥ 25 kg/m² (≥ 23 kg/m² in Asian Americans) and elevated waist circumference (> 102 cm in men, > 88 cm in women).
* **First-Degree Family History:** Parental or sibling history of T2D.
* **Sedentary Lifestyle & Age ≥ 35 years.**
* **History of Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) or Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS).**
* **Race/Ethnicity:** Higher prevalence in Hispanic, Black, Asian American, and Indigenous populations.

Pathophysiology involves peripheral insulin resistance in skeletal muscle and liver, excessive hepatic gluconeogenesis, glucotoxicity, lipotoxicity, and progressive failure of pancreatic islet beta-cells.

## 3. Chronic Complications

Chronic hyperglycemia causes extensive microvascular and macrovascular pathology:

### 3.1 Microvascular Complications
* **Diabetic Retinopathy:** Leading cause of preventable blindness in working-age adults. Features microaneurysms, cotton wool spots, retinal hemorrhages, macular edema, and neovascularization (proliferative retinopathy).
* **Diabetic Kidney Disease (Nephropathy):** Defined by persistent elevated urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (**UACR ≥ 30 mg/g**) and/or sustained reduction in estimated glomerular filtration rate (**eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73m²**).
* **Diabetic Neuropathy:** Most commonly distal symmetric polyneuropathy (glove-and-stocking sensory loss, paresthesias, pain, increased risk of diabetic foot ulcers and amputations) and autonomic neuropathy (gastroparesis, postural hypotension, resting tachycardia).

### 3.2 Macrovascular Complications
* Coronary Artery Disease (CAD / Myocardial Infarction), Ischemic Cerebrovascular Disease (Stroke), and Peripheral Artery Disease (PAD). Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of mortality in patients with T2D.

## 4. Glycemic Targets and Monitoring

* **General Adult HbA1c Target:** **< 7.0% (53 mmol/mol)** for non-pregnant adults without significant hypoglycemia.
* **Stringent Target (< 6.5%):** Appropriate for younger patients with short disease duration, long life expectancy, and no significant ASCVD, if achievable without hypoglycemia.
* **Less Stringent Target (< 8.0%):** Appropriate for elderly frail individuals with history of severe hypoglycemia, limited life expectancy, extensive comorbidities, or advanced microvascular/macrovascular disease.
* **Self-Monitoring:** Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) or capillary fingerstick testing before meals (target 80–130 mg/dL) and 1–2 hours postprandial (target < 180 mg/dL).

## 5. Pharmacological Management

### 5.1 First-Line Therapy
* **Metformin:** (Biguanide, titrated to 1,000–2,000 mg/day). Decreases hepatic glucose production and enhances insulin sensitivity. High glycemic efficacy, weight neutral, low hypoglycemia risk. *Renal dose adjustment needed if eGFR 30–44; contraindicated if eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m².*
* Comprehensive lifestyle intervention (hypocaloric Mediterranean or plant-based diet, ≥ 150 minutes/week moderate aerobic exercise).

### 5.2 Cardiorenal Protective Add-on Agents
* **SGLT2 Inhibitors (Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter-2 Inhibitors):** (e.g., Empagliflozin 10–25 mg, Dapagliflozin 10 mg). Promotes urinary glucose excretion. Proven to reduce heart failure hospitalizations and slow progression of diabetic chronic kidney disease regardless of baseline HbA1c.
* **GLP-1 Receptor Agonists (Glucagon-Like Peptide-1):** (e.g., Semaglutide, Liraglutide, Dulaglutide). Enhances glucose-dependent insulin secretion, slows gastric emptying, promotes significant weight loss, and provides superior reduction in major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE).

### 5.3 Other Agents & Insulin Therapy
* **DPP-4 Inhibitors:** (e.g., Sitagliptin 100 mg daily). Weight neutral, safe, oral.
* **Basal Insulin:** (e.g., Insulin Glargine or Degludec). Initiated when HbA1c remains significantly elevated despite combination non-insulin therapies, or in catabolic presentation (weight loss, ketosis).
