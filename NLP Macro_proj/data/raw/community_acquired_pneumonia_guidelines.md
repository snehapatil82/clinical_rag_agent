# Clinical Guidelines for Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) in Adults

**Source:** Infectious Diseases Society & Thoracic Clinical Consensus  
**Document Code:** IDSA-ATS-CAP-2024  
**Scope:** Adult Acute Respiratory Infection & Antimicrobial Stewardship  

---

## 1. Definition and Pathogenesis

Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP) is an acute infection of the pulmonary parenchyma (alveoli and distal bronchioles) acquired outside of hospital or healthcare facilities.

Pathogenesis begins when microorganisms colonize the upper respiratory tract and are microaspirated into the lower airways, or inhaled as infectious aerosols. Alveolar macrophages initiate an acute inflammatory response, triggering release of cytokines (IL-1, TNF-alpha), neutrophilic recruitment, capillary leakage, and alveolar exudative consolidation that impairs gas exchange (ventilation-perfusion mismatch).

---

## 2. Cardinal Symptoms and Clinical Presentation

Classic clinical features of CAP include:
1. **Acute Respiratory Symptoms:** Cough (frequently productive of purulent or rust-colored sputum), dyspnea, tachypnea, and pleuritic chest pain aggravated by deep inspiration.
2. **Systemic Manifestations:** High fever, rigors/chills, diaphoresis, malaise, fatigue, anorexia, and mental status changes/confusion (especially in geriatric patients).
3. **Physical Examination Signs:** Focal bronchial breath sounds, inspiratory crackles (rales), localized dullness to percussion, increased tactile vocal fremitus, and egophony over the consolidated pulmonary lobe.

---

## 3. Diagnostic Evaluation and Confirmation

* **Chest Radiography (PA and Lateral):** Gold standard diagnostic confirmation showing pulmonary infiltrate, lobar consolidation, or air bronchograms not previously present.
* **Microbiological Testing:**
  * Sputum Gram stain and culture (recommended in severe CAP or hospitalized patients).
  * Blood cultures (2 sets drawn prior to antibiotic administration for severe CAP).
  * Urinary antigen testing for *Legionella pneumophila* serogroup 1 and *Streptococcus pneumoniae*.
  * Multiplex respiratory viral PCR panel (detecting Influenza A/B, RSV, SARS-CoV-2).
* **Biomarkers:** Serum Procalcitonin (PCT) and C-reactive protein (CRP) assist in distinguishing bacterial infection from viral etiologies and assessing clinical resolution.

---

## 4. Etiological Pathogens

* **Typical Bacterial Pathogens:**
  * *Streptococcus pneumoniae* (Pneumococcus): Most common cause across all age groups (~50% of bacterial CAP).
  * *Haemophilus influenzae*: Common in patients with underlying COPD or smoking history.
  * *Moraxella catarrhalis*, *Staphylococcus aureus* (notable post-influenza).
* **Atypical Pathogens:**
  * *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*: Common in adolescents and young adults ("walking pneumonia").
  * *Chlamydia pneumoniae* and *Legionella pneumophila* (associated with water reservoirs/cooling towers).
* **Viral Pathogens:** Influenza virus A and B, Respiratory Syncytial Virus (RSV), SARS-CoV-2, Rhinovirus, Adenovirus.

---

## 5. Severity Assessment: CURB-65 Score

The CURB-65 criteria stratify 30-day mortality risk and guide site-of-care decisions (outpatient vs. inpatient vs. ICU):
* **C:** Confusion (new mental confusion, AMT score ≤ 8). [1 point]
* **U:** Urea (blood urea nitrogen BUN > 19 mg/dL or serum urea > 7 mmol/L). [1 point]
* **R:** Respiratory Rate (≥ 30 breaths/min). [1 point]
* **B:** Blood Pressure (Systolic BP < 90 mmHg OR Diastolic BP ≤ 60 mmHg). [1 point]
* **65:** Age ≥ 65 years. [1 point]

*Management by Score:*
* **Score 0–1 (Low Risk, Mortality < 1.5%):** Outpatient management recommended.
* **Score 2 (Moderate Risk, Mortality ~9%):** Consider brief hospital inpatient stay or supervised outpatient care.
* **Score 3–5 (High Risk, Mortality 15–40%):** Urgent hospitalization; ICU admission evaluation required for scores 4–5.

---

## 6. Empiric Antimicrobial Treatment

### 6.1 Outpatient Therapy (Previously Healthy, No Comorbidities)
* **First-Line:** High-dose **Amoxicillin** (1g orally three times daily) OR **Doxycycline** (100 mg orally twice daily).
* **Alternative:** Macrolide (Azithromycin 500 mg day 1, then 250 mg daily) only in regions with pneumococcal macrolide resistance < 25%.

### 6.2 Outpatient with Comorbidities (COPD, Heart/Kidney/Liver Disease, Diabetes)
* **Combination Therapy:** Beta-lactam (Amoxicillin/Clavulanate 875/125 mg BID or Cefpodoxime 200 mg BID) PLUS Macrolide (Azithromycin) or Doxycycline.
* **Monotherapy:** Respiratory Fluoroquinolone (Levofloxacin 750 mg daily or Moxifloxacin 400 mg daily).

### 6.3 Duration of Therapy
* Standard duration: **5 to 7 days**, provided the patient has achieved clinical stability (afebrile for ≥ 48 hours, normal vital signs, capable of oral intake).
