# European and American Guidelines on Cardiovascular Disease Prevention

**Source:** Task Force for Cardiovascular Disease Prevention in Clinical Practice  
**Document Code:** ESC-ACC-CVD-2024  
**Scope:** Preventive Cardiology, Atherosclerosis & Lipidology  

---

## 1. Pathogenesis of Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Disease (ASCVD)

Atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease encompasses coronary artery disease, ischemic cerebrovascular disease, and peripheral arterial disease. 

The underlying process of atherogenesis involves:
1. Endothelial dysfunction triggered by elevated circulating apolipoprotein B-containing lipoproteins (primarily LDL-C), smoking, hypertension, and systemic inflammation.
2. Subendothelial retention of Low-Density Lipoprotein (LDL) particles and subsequent oxidation.
3. Monocyte recruitment and differentiation into macrophages, forming lipid-laden foam cells.
4. Formation of fatty streaks progressing to fibrous atherosclerotic plaques with necrotic lipid cores.
5. Plaque rupture or erosion provoking acute platelet aggregation, thrombus formation, and vessel occlusion (causing acute myocardial infarction or stroke).

## 2. Risk Factors and Global Risk Estimation

Cardiovascular risk is multiplicative across interacting factors. Risk estimation algorithms (such as SCORE2, ASCVD Pooled Cohort Equations) evaluate 10-year risk based on:
* **Age and Sex:** Risk rises steeply with age; men develop ASCVD ~10 years earlier than women.
* **Dyslipidemia:** Elevated Total Cholesterol, LDL-C, Triglycerides, and low High-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol (HDL-C).
* **Elevated Systolic Blood Pressure.**
* **Cigarette Smoking:** Increases risk of cardiovascular events by 2- to 3-fold.
* **Diabetes Mellitus & Obesity:** Metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance, elevated visceral adiposity.
* **Emerging Biomarkers:** Lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a) > 50 mg/dL or > 125 nmol/L], high-sensitivity C-reactive protein (hs-CRP ≥ 2.0 mg/L), coronary artery calcium (CAC) score > 100 Agatston units.

## 3. Lipid Management and Target LDL-C Thresholds

LDL-C is the primary causal driver of atherosclerosis. Lowering LDL-C yields proportional, linear reductions in cardiovascular events ("lower is better and earlier is better"):

### 3.1 Risk Categories and LDL-C Targets
* **Very High-Risk Patients:** (Established ASCVD, Diabetes with target organ damage, severe CKD eGFR < 30).
  * **Target:** **LDL-C < 55 mg/dL (1.4 mmol/L)** AND at least a **≥ 50% reduction** from baseline LDL-C.
* **High-Risk Patients:** (Markedly elevated single risk factors such as total cholesterol > 310 mg/dL, Diabetes > 10 years duration without organ damage, moderate CKD eGFR 30–59).
  * **Target:** **LDL-C < 70 mg/dL (1.8 mmol/L)** AND ≥ 50% reduction from baseline.
* **Moderate-Risk Patients:**
  * **Target:** **LDL-C < 100 mg/dL (2.6 mmol/L)**.
* **Low-Risk Patients:**
  * **Target:** **LDL-C < 116 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L)**.

### 3.2 Pharmacotherapy for Dyslipidemia
* **First-Line: High-Intensity Statins:** (Atorvastatin 40–80 mg daily, Rosuvastatin 20–40 mg daily). Inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, upregulates hepatic LDL receptors, reduces LDL-C by ≥ 50%.
* **Second-Line: Ezetimibe:** (10 mg daily). Inhibits Niemann-Pick C1-Like 1 (NPC1L1) intestinal cholesterol absorption. Adds an additional 15–20% LDL-C reduction when combined with statins.
* **Third-Line: PCSK9 Inhibitors:** (Evolocumab 140 mg Q2W, Alirocumab 75–150 mg Q2W) or Inclisiran (siRNA). Decreases LDL receptor degradation, lowering LDL-C by an additional 50–60%.

## 4. Lifestyle and Diet Recommendations for Cardiovascular Health

* **Dietary Pattern:** Mediterranean-style or plant-forward dietary pattern rich in fruits, vegetables, legumes, nuts, whole grains, and olive oil. Saturated fatty acids should comprise < 7% of total energy intake, replaced by polyunsaturated and monounsaturated fatty acids. Complete elimination of dietary trans-fats.
* **Physical Activity:** Minimum of 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity or 75–150 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic physical activity weekly.
* **Smoking Cessation:** Complete cessation of tobacco products, electronic cigarettes, and avoidance of secondhand smoke. Smoking cessation reduces cardiovascular risk by ~50% within 1–2 years.
* **Body Weight:** Maintain BMI between 20.0 and 24.9 kg/m² and waist circumference < 94 cm in men, < 80 cm in women.

---

## 5. Antiplatelet Therapy

* **Secondary Prevention:** Low-dose Aspirin (75–100 mg once daily) or Clopidogrel (75 mg once daily) is indicated lifelong in all patients with established ASCVD to prevent secondary ischemic events.
* **Dual Antiplatelet Therapy (DAPT):** Aspirin plus a potent P2Y12 inhibitor (Ticagrelor 90 mg BID or Prasugrel 10 mg daily) for 12 months following Acute Coronary Syndrome (ACS) or percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI).
