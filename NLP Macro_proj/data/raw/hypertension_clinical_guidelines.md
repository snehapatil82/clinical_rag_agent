# Clinical Practice Guidelines for the Management of Arterial Hypertension

**Source:** International Consensus Guidelines for Hypertension Management  
**Document Code:** CPG-HTN-2024  
**Scope:** Adult Clinical Practice & Preventive Cardiology  

---

## 1. Definition and Classification of Blood Pressure

Arterial hypertension is defined as a persistent elevation of systemic arterial blood pressure. Blood pressure (BP) classification in adults is categorized based on properly measured seated office readings:

* **Normal Blood Pressure:** Systolic BP < 120 mmHg and Diastolic BP < 80 mmHg.
* **Elevated Blood Pressure:** Systolic BP 120–129 mmHg and Diastolic BP < 80 mmHg.
* **Stage 1 Hypertension:** Systolic BP 130–139 mmHg or Diastolic BP 80–89 mmHg.
* **Stage 2 Hypertension:** Systolic BP ≥ 140 mmHg or Diastolic BP ≥ 90 mmHg.
* **Hypertensive Crisis:** Systolic BP > 180 mmHg and/or Diastolic BP > 120 mmHg, requiring immediate clinical evaluation for acute target-organ damage.

---

## 2. Etiology and Major Risk Factors

Essential (primary) hypertension accounts for 90–95% of adult cases, arising from a multifactorial interplay of genetic predisposition and environmental factors. Secondary hypertension (5–10%) results from identifiable causes such as renal artery stenosis, chronic kidney disease, primary aldosteronism, or obstructive sleep apnea.

Key modifiable risk factors include:
1. **High Dietary Sodium Intake:** Consuming > 2,300 mg sodium/day (approx. 5.8g salt/day) expands plasma volume and elevates peripheral vascular resistance.
2. **Physical Inactivity & Obesity:** Excess adiposity increases sympathetic nervous system activity and renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) stimulation.
3. **Excessive Alcohol Consumption:** Regular intake of > 2 standard drinks/day in men or > 1 standard drink/day in women.
4. **Chronic Stress & Tobacco Smoking:** Nicotine transiently elevates blood pressure and accelerates atherogenesis.

Non-modifiable risk factors include advancing age (stiffening of large elastic arteries), family history of premature cardiovascular disease, and chronic kidney disease.

---

## 3. Clinical Manifestations and Target-Organ Damage

Hypertension is frequently asymptomatic in early stages ("the silent killer"). When severe or prolonged, symptoms and complications include:
* Morning occipital headaches, dizziness, palpitations, and epistaxis.
* **Target-Organ Complications:** Left ventricular hypertrophy (LVH), coronary heart disease, ischemic or hemorrhagic stroke, hypertensive nephrosclerosis (albuminuria, elevated serum creatinine), and hypertensive retinopathy.

---

## 4. Lifestyle Interventions (Non-Pharmacological Therapy)

Non-pharmacological therapy is mandatory for all patients with elevated BP or hypertension:
* **Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH) Diet:** Emphasizes fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy, and reduced saturated fat. Lowers SBP by approx. 8–14 mmHg.
* **Sodium Restriction:** Target < 1,500 mg/day or at least a 1,000 mg/day reduction. Lowers SBP by approx. 5–6 mmHg.
* **Potassium Supplementation:** Target 3,500–5,000 mg/day through potassium-rich dietary intake (unless contraindicated by chronic kidney disease or hyperkalemia).
* **Physical Activity:** 90–150 minutes/week of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise (or dynamic resistance exercise). Lowers SBP by 4–9 mmHg.
* **Weight Reduction:** Target BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 kg/m². Expect ~1 mmHg SBP drop per 1 kg weight lost.

## 5. Pharmacological Management & Treatment Thresholds

### 5.1 Initiation Thresholds
* **Stage 1 Hypertension with 10-year ASCVD Risk ≥ 10%:** Initiate monotherapy alongside lifestyle modifications.
* **Stage 2 Hypertension:** Initiate therapy immediately, typically with two first-line antihypertensive agents of different pharmacological classes.

### 5.2 First-Line Antihypertensive Classes
1. **Thiazide / Thiazide-like Diuretics:** (e.g., Chlorthalidone 12.5–25 mg daily, Hydrochlorothiazide 25–50 mg daily). Effective at reducing stroke and heart failure events.
2. **Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme Inhibitors (ACEi):** (e.g., Lisinopril 10–40 mg daily, Ramipril 2.5–10 mg daily). Renoprotective in diabetic kidney disease. *Contraindicated in pregnancy.*
3. **Angiotensin Receptor Blockers (ARBs):** (e.g., Losartan 50–100 mg daily, Telmisartan 40–80 mg daily). Used when ACE inhibitors cause intolerable cough. *Contraindicated in pregnancy; do not combine with ACEi.*
4. **Dihydropyridine Calcium Channel Blockers (CCBs):** (e.g., Amlodipine 5–10 mg daily). Highly effective in elderly patients and Black patient populations.

### 5.3 Blood Pressure Goals
* General recommended target for adults with confirmed hypertension: **< 130/80 mmHg**.
* In frail older adults, individualized clinical judgment is advised with a systolic target of < 140 mmHg.
